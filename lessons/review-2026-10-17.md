With $Q$ as you wrote it, the O column gives $dO/dt = 5C - 250\,O$ directly. Your $Q$ was right, so this was a mechanical slip. On the retry you wrote $4C - 200\,O$ correctly.

2. **What justifies $dO/dt \approx 0$.** This is the real gap, and it showed twice today and once on Oct 14. You justified it first by comparing $k_{ch}$ to $k_{cl}$, then by $k_{op} \ll k_{cl}$ "so $U \approx C$". Neither is the reason. Think about timescales. An open site leaves O at total rate $k_{cl}+k_{ch}$, so the open state lives about $1/(k_{cl}+k_{ch})$, a few milliseconds. The closed pool $C$ drains at rate $k_{op}$, lifetime of a fraction of a second. So after any disturbance, $O$ snaps back to its balance value $\dfrac{k_{op}}{k_{cl}+k_{ch}}\,C$ long before $C$ has noticeably moved. That is why you may set its derivative to zero and let $O$ *track* $C$. The comparison is $k_{cl}+k_{ch} \gg k_{op}$.
   - $k_{op} \ll k_{cl}$ is a statement about *how much* is open (little). Smallness alone is not enough: a small pool that relaxed slowly would not be in steady state.
   - $k_{ch}$ versus $k_{cl}$ decides *which exit wins*, hence EX1 versus EX2. It has nothing to do with whether $O$ is in steady state. In today's numbers ($k_{ch} = 50$, $k_{cl} = 200$) the approximation is excellent even though neither exit dominates.
